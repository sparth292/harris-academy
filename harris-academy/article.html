The Fundamentals of Jeet Kune Do

Understanding the art of intercepting fist

Jeet Kune Do (JKD), or "The Way of the Intercepting Fist," is the martial art and philosophy developed by the legendary Bruce Lee. Rather than being a fixed style with a prescribed set of techniques, JKD is a philosophy of martial arts that emphasizes practicality, flexibility, speed, and efficiency.

At its core, JKD rejects traditional forms and patterns in favor of what works in actual combat. Bruce Lee famously advised his students to "absorb what is useful, discard what is useless, and add what is specifically your own." This philosophy makes JKD one of the most adaptable martial arts systems in existence.

Key Principles

Efficiency: An attack that hits its mark with the least amount of movement and energy is the most effective. JKD strips away unnecessary motions, focusing on directness.

Directness: The shortest distance between two points is a straight line. JKD techniques are designed to go from point A to point B with no wasted motion.

Simplicity: Complex techniques often fail under pressure. JKD favors simple, reliable techniques that can be executed under stress.

Stop Hit & Interception: The defining concept of JKD — intercepting an opponent's attack before it fully develops. This is the highest level of timing in combat.

Training Approach

Training in JKD involves a combination of footwork drills, pad work, sparring, and sensitivity training. Unlike traditional martial arts that rely heavily on static forms (katas), JKD practitioners spend most of their time developing attributes like timing, distance management, and reactive ability through dynamic drills.

The five ranges of combat in JKD — kicking, punching, trapping, grappling, and weapons — ensure that a practitioner is prepared for any situation. This comprehensive approach is what makes JKD such a complete fighting system.
